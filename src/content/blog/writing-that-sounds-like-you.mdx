AI writing tools are great at getting words on the page. Where they fall short is
rhythm — sentence after sentence lands at roughly the same length, with the same
handful of transition words doing all the work.

Human writing doesn't work that way. A short sentence lands hard. A longer one
carries a thought through a turn or two. That variation is what makes text feel
like it came from a person who was actually thinking as they wrote, not filling in
a template.

A few things to look for when you're editing:

- **Repeated sentence openers.** If three sentences in a row start with "It is" or
  "This means," that's a signal to restructure.
- **Hedging filler.** Phrases like "it is important to note that" or "in today's
  fast-paced world" rarely add meaning — cut them.
- **Uniform length.** Mix short, punchy sentences with longer ones that carry more
  than one idea.
