We build one of the two tools in this comparison, which is a conflict of interest worth stating in the first sentence rather than the last. Our [editorial standards](/about/editorial) commit us to naming our own weaknesses in pieces like this, and there are several below.

The genuinely useful thing to say here is not which tool is better. It is that these two tools do different jobs, and most people arrive at this comparison without having worked out which job they need doing.

## Two different operations

**Paraphrasing** takes a passage and restates it in different words while keeping the meaning. The operation is substitution: synonyms swapped, clauses reordered, active flipped to passive. The output says what the input said, in different words.

**Rhythm rewriting** takes a passage and changes its *shape*: sentence lengths, paragraph structure, where emphasis falls, which filler gets cut. The words may be largely the same. What changes is the pattern they arrive in.

QuillBot is a paraphraser with a humanizing mode built on that foundation. Humanetext's [Text Humanizer](/humanize-text) is built around the structural patterns that make prose read as machine-written — uniform sentence length, repeated openers, hedging phrases, symmetrical paragraphs.

That difference determines almost everything else, so it is worth being sure which one your problem is.

## Which problem do you have?

Take a paragraph that is bothering you and ask: is the issue the *words*, or the *rhythm*?

**A words problem** sounds like: this is too close to my source and I need to restate it. This register is wrong for the audience. I have used "significant" four times. I need this shorter.

**A rhythm problem** sounds like: every sentence is the same length. This reads like a report. It is technically fine and completely lifeless. I cannot say what is wrong but it does not sound like a person wrote it.

Paraphrasing fixes the first and can make the second worse, because substituting synonyms inside an unchanged sentence structure leaves the structure — which was the problem — untouched. You get different words in the same monotone.

Rhythm rewriting fixes the second and does very little for the first. If you needed the passage restated to avoid echoing a source, changing its cadence does not accomplish that.

Most people searching for a "humanizer" have a rhythm problem and reach for a paraphraser, because paraphrasing is the better-known category.

## What QuillBot does well

Genuine strengths, from a tool with a long track record.

**Paraphrasing at the clause level.** This is its core competence and it is good at it. Restating a passage without echoing the original's phrasing is a real task with real uses — summarising sources, avoiding accidental duplication, rewriting something you drafted badly.

**Control over the operation.** Multiple modes — standard, fluency, formal, and others on paid tiers — plus a synonym slider that lets you set how aggressively it substitutes. That granularity matters when you need a light touch on a technical passage where changing terminology would introduce errors.

**A suite rather than a single tool.** Grammar checking, summarising, citation generation, plagiarism checking, and browser and document integrations. If you want one subscription covering several jobs, that is a real advantage.

**Maturity.** It has been around a long time, the interface is stable, and the behaviour is predictable. Ours is newer and narrower.

## What the Text Humanizer does differently

**It targets structure, not vocabulary.** It looks for uniform sentence length, repeated sentence openers, hedging constructions, and evenly-weighted paragraphs — the patterns catalogued in [12 sentence patterns that make prose sound machine-made](/blog/sentence-patterns-that-sound-machine-made) — and changes those. A rewrite may keep most of your nouns and verbs while reading completely differently, because what moved was the shape.

**It cuts rather than substituting.** Hedging openers, empty participial phrases, and summary conclusions get deleted rather than reworded. Paraphrasers tend to preserve length, because their operation is substitution; a filler phrase goes in and a different filler phrase comes out.

**No account, and no paid tier at all.** Three rewrites a day, renewed daily, tracked by an anonymous browser identifier. An optional email raises the limit. There is currently nothing to buy.

**A photo tool alongside it.** The [Photo Humanizer](/humanize-photo) adds camera-like texture to overly clean images. QuillBot has no equivalent, though it is only relevant if you have that problem.

## Where ours is worse

The part that makes this comparison worth reading.

**The daily limit is genuinely restrictive.** Three rewrites a day will not get you through a document. QuillBot's free tier, and certainly its paid tier, will handle far more volume. If you are working at any scale, this is disqualifying and we would rather you knew now.

**No modes, no control.** You get one rewriting behaviour. There is no formality slider, no aggressiveness setting, no way to say "light touch on this technical paragraph." QuillBot's granularity is a real advantage and we do not have an answer to it.

**Long documents are not the use case.** It works on passages. Paste in four thousand words and you will hit the limits of both the daily quota and the processing.

**No integrations.** No browser extension, no Word add-in, no API. Copy and paste is the entire interface.

**It can flatten technical precision.** Rewriting for rhythm sometimes replaces an exact term with a smoother one. On technical or legal writing, read the output carefully — this is the failure mode to watch for, and it is the same warning we would give about any rewriting tool including QuillBot's.

**It is newer.** Less battle-testing, fewer edge cases found, a smaller team.

## The detection question

This is what most people are actually asking, so it deserves a direct answer rather than a marketing one.

Neither tool can reliably guarantee that rewritten text will pass any given AI detector, and any tool claiming otherwise is selling you something it cannot deliver. Detectors measure statistical predictability, they change without notice, they disagree with each other, and they produce false positives on ordinary human writing at meaningful rates. We go through the mechanism in [how AI content detectors actually work](/blog/how-ai-content-detectors-work).

You will find comparison articles ranking humanizers by detector-evasion scores. Treat those with caution. The scores are snapshots against specific detector versions at specific moments, they are frequently produced by parties with an interest in the result, and they measure a target that moves weekly.

More importantly, evasion is not our design goal and we do not want it to be. Using either tool to disguise work you did not do is against our [terms](/terms), and if you have been flagged for something you wrote yourself, rewriting it after the accusation is the worst available move — we explain why in [defending your work against an AI accusation](/blog/falsely-accused-of-using-ai).

## A straight recommendation

**Use QuillBot if** you need to restate source material without echoing it, you want control over how aggressive the rewriting is, you are working through volume, you want grammar checking and citation tools in the same subscription, or you need it inside your browser or word processor.

**Use the Text Humanizer if** your problem is specifically that a draft reads flat and machine-made, you want the structural filler cut rather than reworded, and three rewrites a day is enough for what you are doing.

**Use both if** that is what the work needs. They are not really competitors. Paraphrase first to get the wording where you want it, then fix the rhythm. Nothing stops you.

**Use neither if** you have twenty minutes and a draft you care about. Read it aloud, cut every hedging opener, break up the runs of same-length sentences, and replace one abstraction with a specific example. That gets you most of the way, for free, permanently, and it improves your writing rather than just this document. The [editing checklist](/blog/editing-checklist-for-ai-generated-content) covers the sequence.

The most useful comparison is not this article. Take a paragraph that is actually bothering you, run it through both, and read all three versions aloud. Ten minutes on your own text beats anyone's claims about either tool — including ours.
